How the traffic pattern works

A traffic pattern is the standard path airplanes fly around an airport to take off and land. When every pilot flies the same shape at the same height, everyone knows where to look for everyone else.

  • Pattern altitude is usually 1,000 ft above the airport. Pine Ridge sits at 700 ft, so you’ll fly the pattern at 1,700 ft on your altimeter.
  • Take off and land into the wind. The windsock shows you which way it’s blowing.
  • Most patterns use left turns (left traffic). Some runways use right traffic. The segmented circle around the windsock shows which way to turn.
  • Talk on the radio. Pine Ridge has no control tower, so pilots tell each other where they are on a shared frequency called CTAF.
  • Arriving? Join on a 45° angle to the middle of the downwind leg, already at pattern altitude.

Traffic pattern direction

Left traffic is standard. Set right traffic to practice a runway that uses right turns. The wind picks which runway you’ll use, and it changes every flight.
